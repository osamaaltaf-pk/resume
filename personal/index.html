Hi, I'm Osama.

Founder of Wisko · AI engineer · future Master's student in AI

I trained as a physicist and ended up building AI systems. These days I spend most of my time on Wisko, a small company I started where agents turn an idea into a hand-drawn whiteboard video. Before that I worked remotely with teams in London, Miami, and Morocco on voice agents, real-time translation, and serving large language models.

What keeps pulling me in is the gap between a model that sounds right and one that is actually right, and why that gap gets so much harder once real users, many agents, and real latency budgets are involved. I want to study it properly, which is why my next step is a Master's in AI.

What I want to learn next

Building products taught me where my understanding runs out. These are the questions I want to spend my Master's on:

LLMs and VLMs that can show their work

When I fine-tuned Llama 3.2 Vision on radiography, it wrote fluent, report-like text almost immediately. Proving that the text came from a specific region of the image was much harder. I want to understand grounding in vision-language models, not just fluency.

Many agents, many users

Wisko runs a chain of agents for many users at once, and most of what goes wrong lives in the handoffs: shared state, memory, who is allowed to see what. I want to study multi-agent systems and multi-user LLMs as a research problem, not only an engineering one.

Inference, and learning at inference time

Most of my working hours have gone into making models fast enough to use: KV caching, quantization, batching, sub-second voice pipelines. I want to go deeper into efficient inference and into how models can reason and adapt while they're being used.

How I got here

I did my BS in Physics at KFUEIT (2019 to 2023), where I fell for the computational side. Somewhere between lab reports I helped organize three international conferences and the region's first Science Mela, which taught me that I like making things happen as much as studying them.

My final-year project tried to detect cotton-leaf disease with a Vision Transformer and a homemade AI microscope. It worked on the test set and was confidently wrong on leaves it had never seen. That lesson about data and labels has followed me into everything since.

After graduating I joined a small software house as a junior AI engineer, then went remote: a voice-agent platform for a US client, a government system that translates speech across 30+ languages in under a second and a half, and LLM serving work for a team in London. Now I'm putting all of that into Wisko.
